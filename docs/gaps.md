> Companion to `docs/y3172_mapping.md` and `docs/readiness_mapping.md`. The machine-readable register is `kb/gaps/gap_register.json` (generated by `kb/gaps/export_gaps.py` from `kb/gaps/seed_gaps.yaml`; runtime gaps written by the GapAnalystAgent are merged in by `--db`/`--runtime`). **Naming.** Gap ids P1–P12 are policy gaps, S1–S8 are *standards* gaps; scenarios are also called S1–S7 (data/scenarios/S*.yaml). In the *Surfaced by* columns the ids are **scenarios**; in the scenario matrix standards gaps are written `S1(std)`.

**How to read this document.** *Finding* = what the texts we could read say and do not say. *Verified* means read in a downloaded text (Law 2024/017 via OCR of the Presidency's certified copy); *as reported* means taken from a press or secondary page (`kind: summary`). "Not found" means **not found in the texts reviewed on 10–11 Nov 2026**, not proof of absence. Proposals marked *(proposal)* are ours, not law.

## 1. Corrections and limits made while building the KB (read first)

1. **P1 corrected.** The first seed claimed Law 2024/017 "reportedly does not address automated decision-making". The primary text contains **Art. 44** (right to object to decisions based exclusively on automated processing; exceptions; human intervention, point of view, contest). The gap is therefore *operational* (what is a decision, who is accountable, proactive vs on-request review, duty to explain), not total absence. The secondary summaries the seed relied on were incomplete.
2. **Law 2024/017 is read from OCR.** The only public copy we reached is a scanned certified copy with a diagonal watermark; articles 1–75 were recovered after binarisation, but OCR errors are possible. Check the Journal officiel before any legal use.
3. **Treaties.** The Addis Ababa convention's UN registration record lists 12 Parties and **Cameroon is not one of them** (Cameroon appears on the signature pages). Cameroon's ratification of the Malabo Convention was not verified. Both are therefore not treated as binding on Cameroon (see P10).
4. **Missing text.** Law 2023/007 (higher-education orientation) could not be obtained (P8). The official SNIA document and the UAE PDPL text are also missing (overview pages only); UNESCO/ISO/CoE pages answer HTTP 403 to our crawler and are listed for manual drop-in (`kb/README.md`).
5. **Status of the Data Protection Authority** (operational? adequacy list? standard clauses?) is known only from secondary sources (as reported) and must be confirmed with the Authority before relying on P4.

## 3. Policy gaps in detail
### P1 — Automated-decision safeguards exist in law (Art. 44) but are not operational for AI-assisted document verification

*Severity:* high · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S2, S6

**Finding.** Law 2024/017 Art. 44 gives the data subject the right to object to any decision based exclusively on automated processing, with exceptions (informed + explicit consent; authorised by law with safeguards) and the rights to obtain human intervention, to express a point of view and to contest the decision. What is NOT defined anywhere we could read: (a) whether an INCONSISTENT/INSUFFICIENT verdict used by an employer or ministry is a 'decision'; (b) who is the controller of an AI verdict (verifier, registrar, issuing university); (c) whether human review is proactive or only on request; (d) any duty to explain which evidence drove the verdict. There is no AI statute or AI authority yet (SNIA, as reported, announces a framework law).

**Correction vs first seed.** The first seed said Law 2024/017 'reportedly does not address automated decision-making'. The primary text (OCR of the Presidency's certified copy) contains Art. 44, so the gap is operational detail, not total absence.

**Law / text cited.**
- Art. 44(1)-(2) — right to object to exclusively automated decisions; exceptions (`CM-DP-2024#c049`, binding, primary)
- Art. 44(3) — human intervention, point of view, contest (`CM-DP-2024#c049`, binding, primary)
- SNIA pillar 1 (as reported) — AI Authority, Presidential Council on AI and framework law announced, not yet enacted (`CM-SNIA-2025#c001`, non-binding, summary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** rule PR-REGISTRAR-001; rule PR-HUMAN-REVIEW-001; scenario S2; scenario S6

**Proposal.** Make human review of every adverse or uncertain AI verdict mandatory and proactive for employment/admission uses, with a written explanation of the evidence used (rules PR-REGISTRAR-001, PR-HUMAN-REVIEW-001, both proposal: true); define the registrar as the accountable person.

**Comparison.** EU: GDPR Art. 22 and AI Act Art. 14 (human oversight) and Art. 86 (right to explanation; education systems are high-risk, Annex III) — comparison only, not binding in Cameroon. AU Malabo Convention Art. 14 states the same 'no decision based solely on automated processing' principle (Cameroon's ratification NOT verified).

**Verification.** Art. 44 read in the OCR text (primary). SNIA content as reported by OECD.AI / Business in Cameroon (secondary).

### P2 — Probative value of online verification results and of digitised/OCR copies of archives is undefined

*Severity:* high · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S5

**Finding.** Law 2024/001 Art. 29 gives a certified copy produced by the national archive body the same evidential admission as the original; Art. 19(2) leaves the management/conservation of electronic archives to future regulation; Law 2010/021 Art. 13 allows an electronic writing under Civil Code conditions; Law 2010/012 declares a 'legal regime of digital evidence' (Section 1). None of these texts addresses (i) a scan/OCR copy held by a university or exam board, (ii) the output of an automated verification platform or of a tool such as VeriDiplo, (iii) hashing / chain of custody of an evidence pack.

**Law / text cited.**
- Law 2024/001 Art. 29 — certified copy = original, only when produced by the national archive body (`CM-ARCH-2024#c031`, binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** rule PR-EVIDENCE-001; scenario S5; evidence-pack integrity hash (ReporterAgent)

**Proposal.** MINESUP and the archive body define the evidential status of digitised copies and verification results, mandatory hashing and chain-of-custody metadata (PROV-O), and which institution may certify an evidence pack.

**Comparison.** UAE: verification is performed by officially appointed agencies (Dataflow, QuadraBay) that contact the issuing university directly and issue a final report through the ministry portal (The National, 2025, as reported) — evidential value attaches to the agency/ministry report, not to the document copy.

**Verification.** Articles read in the downloaded texts; absence of other provisions = 'not found in the texts reviewed', not proof of absence.

### P3 — No published retention schedule for verification requests and uploaded diplomas

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S1

**Finding.** Law 2024/017 Art. 13 forbids keeping data longer than necessary and says the period is stated in the processing authorisation; Art. 28 says the maximum period is set in the Authority's reference framework (référentiel, Art. 27). We found no such schedule published, and the Authority's operational status is reported as uncertain (see P4).

**Law / text cited.**
- Art. 13 — no longer than necessary; period fixed in authorisation / by regulation (`CM-DP-2024#c018`, binding, primary)
- Art. 28 — maximum period set in the Authority's référentiel (`CM-DP-2024#c033`, binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** rule PR-RETENTION-001 (proposal: true); scenario S1

**Proposal.** Default 90 days then deletion of uploads (OUR proposal, not law); keep only the hashed audit record. To be replaced by the Authority's schedule once published.

**Comparison.** Not compared (no UAE schedule read).

**Verification.** Articles read (OCR). Non-existence of a schedule is 'not found', to confirm with the Authority.

### P4 — Cross-border academic verification depends on a prior authorisation regime that cannot yet be exercised

*Severity:* high · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S4

**Finding.** Law 2024/017 Art. 32 subordinates any transfer of personal data to a foreign State or international organisation to the prior authorisation of the Authority, checking adequate protection, a signed legal instrument with the destination country, binding security rules and Authority-published standard contractual clauses. Art. 53 gives the Authority the task of publishing the list of countries recognised as adequate. Secondary sources consulted 10 Nov 2026 report that implementing decrees / the Authority's operation were still pending (as reported, to verify) and that the law applies after an 18-month transition (23 June 2026). We found no published adequacy list, no standard clauses and no simplified route for a yes/no attestation. Cloud-LLM calls (S4/LLM provider) are transfers too.

**Law / text cited.**
- Art. 32 — prior authorisation for transfers abroad (`CM-DP-2024#c037`, binding, primary)
- Art. 53 — Authority issues authorisations and publishes the list of adequate countries (`CM-DP-2024#c058`, binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** rule PR-CROSSBORDER-001; rule PR-CROSSBORDER-002; rule PR-LLM-TRANSFER-001; scenario S4

**Proposal.** Simplified regime for minimal yes/no attestations (+ hash, no personal data beyond the holder's own claim) with a transfer log; until the Authority issues authorisations, only such attestations leave Cameroon (rules PR-CROSSBORDER-001/002).

**Comparison.** UAE: federal PDPL 45/2021 sets requirements for cross-border transfer (u.ae overview, summary); degree verification is delegated to two appointed agencies with UAE PASS login and a ministry-issued report; MoHRE launched a digital employer verification service (Gulf News, May 2025, as reported). AU Malabo Art. 14 and ECOWAS Supplementary Act give an adequacy-based model (not binding on Cameroon).

**Verification.** Art. 32/53 read (OCR). Authority status and 18-month transition = secondary sources (as_reported).

### P5 — Holder consent is required for MINESUP equivalence/authentication, but no general rule governs employer-initiated checks or bulk queries

*Severity:* high · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S3

**Finding.** The MINESUP equivalence form carries an 'Autorisation de recherche d'informations' by which the holder authorises the Minister to carry out the searches needed to authenticate the diploma. Law 2024/017 Art. 9 requires prior, free, informed, specific and unambiguous consent, with derogations (legal obligation, public-interest mission, vital interests) that leave open whether an employer's 'legitimate interest' qualifies; Art. 50 prohibits processing without consent. Nothing limits the number of holders an HR firm can check, and no consent format (paper form only) exists for digital checks.

**Law / text cited.**
- Art. 9 — consent + derogations (`CM-DP-2024#c014`, binding, primary)
- Art. 50 — processing without consent forbidden (`CM-DP-2024#c055`, binding, primary)
- MINESUP form — holder authorisation to the Minister only (`CM-MINESUP-FORM#c001`, binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** rule PR-CONSENT-001; rule PR-CONSENT-003; rule PR-BULK-001 (20 holders/hour, proposal); scenario S3

**Proposal.** A consent token or an explicit statutory legal basis for every check; legal basis accepted only from public authorities; rate limit per verifier (20 holders/hour, our proposal).

**Comparison.** UAE: employer verification goes through the ministry platform / appointed agencies; the holder logs in with UAE PASS (as reported).

**Verification.** Form and Art. 9/50 read in the downloaded texts.

### P6 — Liability and wording for AI false positives are undefined; outputs may pre-empt a criminal qualification

*Severity:* high · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S2, S7

**Finding.** Art. 62 of Law 2024/017 gives the data subject urgent judicial measures and a claim for reparation, but no text allocates liability between verifier, registrar, tool provider and issuing institution when an automated INCONSISTENT verdict is wrong. The Penal Code defines forgery (Art. 144, 314-315) and unlawful use of an official diploma (Art. 219) as offences for courts to establish; no text regulates how verification tools may word their findings. The stakes are real: press reports ~1,000 gendarmerie trainees dismissed in 2022 and 1,312 diplomas reported as fake in June 2024 (as reported).

**Law / text cited.**
- Art. 62 — judicial remedy and reparation (`CM-DP-2024#c067`, binding, primary)
- Penal Code Art. 219 — unlawful use of an official diploma (`CM-PEN-2016#c003`, binding, primary)
- Penal Code Art. 144 — forgery in a public act (`CM-PEN-2016#c001`, binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** rule PR-NOACCUSE-001; rule PR-APPROVAL-AUDIT-001; scenario S2; scenario S7

**Proposal.** Shared-accountability clause (registrar approves, tool provider logs, verifier acts only on approved verdicts); never output 'fake'; hash-chained audit log as evidence of who decided what.

**Comparison.** UAE: findings are 'authenticated or not' reports issued by the ministry after the agency contacts the university (as reported); no automated accusation.

**Verification.** Articles read in downloaded texts; dismissal figures from press (as_reported).

### P7 — No published legal basis, access conditions or holder rights for the national online diploma-verification platform

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S1, S3

**Finding.** Press (as reported) says the government platform launched on 1 Oct 2024 (MINESUP/MINFOPRA) rests on an agreement of 6 Apr 2023 and a joint circular of 8 May 2024 under which MINESUP gives MINFOPRA a secure software interface to diploma data, to be extended to MINESEC, MINEDUB and MINEFOP. We found no published text on who may query it (private employers?), on logging, on holder notification or on rectification, nor on interoperability with third-party tools.

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** source CM-VERIF-PLATFORM-2024 (summary); scenario S1; scenario S3

**Proposal.** Publish the circular and an access policy (who, purpose, logging, holder notice, SLA) and an open API/credential format so that third-party tools such as VeriDiplo can federate instead of duplicating.

**Comparison.** UAE: ministries publish the attestation/verification workflow and fees online (The National, MoE news, as reported).

**Verification.** Press only (kind: summary, as_reported). The circular itself was not obtained.

### P9 — No deadline or structured-format duty for institutions to answer verification requests or export deliberation registers

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S5

**Finding.** Law 2024/001 Art. 30(3) requires reasoned refusals of archive communication and Art. 19(2) defers electronic-archive modalities to regulation, but we found no deadline for issuers (universities, Office du Baccalauréat, GCE Board) to confirm a diploma, and no duty to hold deliberation minutes (PV) as structured/digitised records. Missing or unreadable archives (S5) therefore end in 'insufficient evidence' with no escalation path.

**Law / text cited.**
- Law 2024/001 Art. 29 — evidence by certified copies from the national archive body (`CM-ARCH-2024#c031`, binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** scenario S5; rule PR-EVIDENCE-001

**Proposal.** Response deadline for issuers, mandatory digitised + structured result registers, and an archive-request template (the system drafts it in S5).

**Comparison.** UAE: the agency contacts the university and the duration depends on the university's response (The National, as reported) — same bottleneck, partially managed by SLAs on the agency side.

**Verification.** Articles read; absence of a deadline = not found in texts reviewed.

### P10 — Binding status for Cameroon of the African recognition and data-protection conventions is not established

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S4

**Finding.** The Addis Ababa revised recognition convention (Art. IV.2(3): Parties commit to eradicate fraudulent practices regarding qualifications; network exchange of counter-fraud measures) is binding only on Parties. The UN registration record in the KB lists 12 Parties and Cameroon is not among them (it appears on the signature pages). Cameroon's ratification of the AU Malabo Convention was not verified.

**Law / text cited.**
- Addis Convention Art. IV.2(3) — commitment to eradicate fraudulent practices (`INT-ADDIS-2014#c011`, non-binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** source INT-ADDIS-2014 (OCR of UN record, pages 1-2 and 12-22); source INT-MALABO-2014

**Proposal.** Confirm status with UNESCO/AU; if Cameroon is not a Party, ratify/accede so that cross-border verification and counter-fraud cooperation have a treaty basis.

**Comparison.** Global Convention (UNESCO 2019) and Lisbon Convention not downloaded (pending) — compare once available.

**Verification.** Party list read in OCR of the UN record (dated registration 2020; may be outdated).

### P11 — No reconciliation procedure for name variants and OCR/transcription errors between civil status, diplomas and deliberation minutes

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S6

**Finding.** Law 2024/017 Art. 12 requires accurate, up-to-date data and a duty to correct inaccurate data, and the rights chapter lets the holder obtain rectification; nothing describes how a holder whose compound name, name order or spelling differs across civil-status documents, diplomas and PVs obtains a documented reconciliation, nor what a verifier may do meanwhile.

**Law / text cited.**
- Art. 12 — accuracy and correction duty (`CM-DP-2024#c017`, binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** scenario S6; rule PR-HUMAN-REVIEW-001; rule PR-HOLDER-NOTICE-001

**Proposal.** Fuzzy-match results between 80 and 92 are 'unknown' and go to human review with a rectification path offered to the holder (rules PR-HUMAN-REVIEW-001, PR-HOLDER-NOTICE-001).

**Comparison.** UAE: a mandatory 'name change document' (legal letter, birth/marriage certificate or earlier passport) is required when the certificate name differs from the passport (The National, as reported).

**Verification.** Art. 12 read; comparison from press.

### P12 — No AI statute, AI authority or regulatory-sandbox regime yet: pilots like this one have no legal status

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S7

**Finding.** SNIA (July 2025, as reported by OECD.AI) plans a Cameroonian AI Authority, a Presidential Council on AI and a framework law; none was found enacted. Without a sandbox regime there is no framework that tells a pilot what data it may use, who supervises it, or when it may go live.

**Law / text cited.**
- SNIA pillar 1 (as reported) — authority, council and framework law announced (`CM-SNIA-2025#c001`, non-binding, summary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** all scenarios run in the sandbox profile; readiness dimension 10 (AI & Policies), metric d10_m01 Policy Sandbox

**Proposal.** A policy-sandbox decree defining admissible synthetic/real data, supervisor, exit criteria and publication of sandbox findings (policy case briefs, d10_m08).

**Comparison.** Not compared (no regulatory-sandbox text in the KB).

**Verification.** SNIA = secondary sources (kind: summary).

### S2 — No standard format for policy-as-code linked to legal provisions

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S2, S3

**Finding.** Rules cite legal text only in prose. VeriDiplo links each rule to chunk ids/anchors of a machine-readable KB and tags proposals vs law.

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** kb/policy_rules/*.yaml; tests/unit/test_policy_rules.py

**Proposal.** Propose the YAML rule schema + context contract (kb/policy_rules/context_schema.yaml) and chunk/anchor format to ITU study groups.

**Comparison.** OECD/NIST/ISO 42001 describe governance processes, not an executable rule format (ISO text not accessible: pending).

**Verification.** see sources

### S3 — ITU-T Y.3172 has no provenance/evidence node, no human-approval gate and a minimal security clause; its P node acts only on model output

*Severity:* high · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S2, S3, S4, S7

**Finding.** Y.3172 clause 8.1 defines P as the node that 'enables the application of policies to the output of the model node' (example: network-safety rules). It sits between M and D, so it cannot govern ingestion (SRC/C), search, or export decisions, carries no consent/purpose concept, no human approval of an output, no provenance/evidence record and no explainability requirement. Clause 9 (security) is two sentences (authentication/authorisation, prevent unauthorised access and data leaking). The Recommendation targets ML in IMT-2020 networks; applying it to document verification is our adaptation.

**Law / text cited.**
- Clause 8.1 — definition of the P node (`ITU-Y3172#c020`, non-binding, primary)
- Clause 9 — security considerations (`ITU-Y3172#c027`, non-binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** docs/y3172_mapping.md; rules guard nodes SRC/C/M/D/SINK (guards_node)

**Proposal.** Add to Y.3172 a P-node sub-function 'evidence and approval gate' (checkpoints before ingest/search/report/share/export, human approval, audit hash chain) and a provenance requirement on C/PP.

**Comparison.** EU AI Act Art. 12 (record-keeping), 14 (human oversight) cover what Y.3172 lacks, for AI systems in general.

**Verification.** see sources

### S6 — AI Readiness 2.0 has no metric for multi-agent coordination, human approval gates or evidence-pack integrity

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S2, S7

**Finding.** Readiness 2.0 (13 dimensions, machine-readable in kb/framework/dimensions.json) contains related metrics — d8_m01 'Co-creation Ease (human in inference process)', d10_m07 'Explainability Metrics', d10_m09 'Audit Metrics', d6_m06 'Safety Integration' — but none measures coordination of several agents under a policy guardian, the rate of human-approved vs auto-released adverse outputs, or integrity/provenance of evidence.

**Law / text cited.**
- Dimension 10 — AI & Policies metrics (`ITU-READINESS-2#c011`, non-binding, primary)
- Dimension 8 — Collaboration with AI metrics (`ITU-READINESS-2#c009`, non-binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** docs/readiness_mapping.md

**Proposal.** Add metrics: human-gate coverage (% adverse outputs human-approved), audit-chain verifiability, proposal-vs-law rule ratio, evidence-linked-output ratio.

**Verification.** see sources

### S7 — No interoperable format for cross-border verification results (yes/no attestation + hash)

*Severity:* medium · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S4

**Finding.** Verification results are currently reports on national portals/agency accounts; no portable, minimal, verifiable result format was found. The Addis Convention foresees an African network exchanging counter-fraud information but gives no technical format.

**Law / text cited.**
- Addis Convention Art. IV.2(3) — counter-fraud cooperation (`INT-ADDIS-2014#c011`, non-binding, primary)
- VC 2.0 section 8.9 — minimal disclosure (`W3C-VC-2.0#c022`, non-binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** rules PR-CROSSBORDER-001/002; scenario S4

**Proposal.** Define a minimal verification-attestation credential (verdict class, institution, session, record hash, issuer signature) as a W3C VC 2.0 profile for AU/ECOWAS/CEMAC use.

**Comparison.** UAE agencies deliver proprietary PDF reports via UAE PASS portals (as reported).

**Verification.** see sources

### S8 — No shared vocabulary for verification outcomes (consistent / inconsistent / insufficient evidence) or for non-accusatory wording

*Severity:* low · *Status:* open · *Surfaced by:* S2, S5

**Finding.** W3C VC 2.0 offers an 'evidence' property (section 5.6) for supporting information but no outcome vocabulary; national platforms report 'authentic or not' (as reported).

- VC 2.0 — related section; the 'evidence' property is section 5.6 (`W3C-VC-2.0#c022`, non-binding, primary)

**Evidence (what surfaced it).** rule PR-NOACCUSE-001; scenarios S2, S5

**Proposal.** Standardise a three-state verdict with mandatory evidence references and a neutral wording list; publish it with the KB format.

**Comparison.** UAE reports a binary authenticated / not authenticated result (as reported).
